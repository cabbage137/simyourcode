    Build your bot
    
    
    Every choice changes how the robot behaves on the field and which device names your code can use. Changing the bot resets the field.
    
    
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Identity
    Category 1
    
          
          
    The alliance colour (red or blue) is picked on the Drive & Code tab and shows as the outline of your robot.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Drivetrain
    Category 2
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    A faster motor or a bigger wheel means more speed but less pushing power and less precise moves.
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Chassis size
    Category 3
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    A small robot slips through gaps; a wide one has a wider intake and is steadier. Nothing may start bigger than 18 in.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Scoring tools
    Category 4
    
          
    Tick the mechanisms you want. Each one uses motors and servos from your limit of 8 + 8.
    
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Sensors
    Category 5
    
          
    Sensors let your code see where the robot is, which is what makes autonomous accurate.

      Step by step: from zero to a full match
      
    
      Follow these in order the first time. Each one takes a couple of minutes.
      
    
        
      
      1. Look at the field
        
        
        Open Drive & Code. You are looking down at the 12 ft × 12 ft field. The red alliance stands on the left, blue on the right. In the middle is the HIVE structure with a red HIVE and a blue HIVE. Yellow balls are POLLEN, red and blue balls are NECTAR. The four pink shapes on the walls are FLOWERS, and the row of small circles beside each one shows what is stacked inside it.
        2. 
      
      2. Build a bot
        
        
        Open Build Bot. Pick a drivetrain, a motor, a wheel size and the tools you want. The preview and the checklist update as you click. The Robot configuration table lists the device names, exactly like the configuration screen on a real Robot Controller. Your code must use these names.
        3. 
      
      3. Open an example program
        
        
        Back on Drive & Code, look at the Robot Controller Console. The Blocks and OnBotJava tabs each list your op modes, sorted into groups. You start with four examples. Press Examples for more, each with a name and a line saying what it does. Examples are written for the bot you have right now, so add a fresh one after you change your bot.
        4. 
      
      4. Run it and drive
        
        
        Open a TeleOp program and press ▶ Run open program. Click anywhere outside the code box, then drive with WASD and the arrow keys, or drag the on-screen sticks. With the example TeleOp: E runs the intake, hold C to spin the launcher and tap K to shoot, T/G move the lift and J opens the gate. Press ■ Stop when you are done.
        5. 
      
      5. Watch the instruments
        
        
        Telemetry is the Driver Station screen: whatever your code sends with telemetry.addData. Motors & servos shows the power going to each motor, its encoder count and each servo position, live. If the robot does something odd, look here first, just as you would on a real robot.
        6. 
      
      6. Change the code
        
        
        Edit the Java in the box, or click blocks in a Blocks program, then press Run again. Try changing LAUNCH_POWER and see how far the shot goes. If you make a mistake, the box under the editor tells you the line and what is wrong. Your work is saved in this browser automatically.
        7. 
      
      7. Score your first HIVE TIP
        
        
        Your upward CELL starts with 3 NECTAR in it. Launch 3 POLLEN into that CELL and the HIVE tips: 20 points, and your human player gets to enter one more NECTAR. After a tip the other CELL faces up and needs 8 POLLEN (or 3 POLLEN and 3 NECTAR) to tip again. Drive around to the other side to reach it.
        8. 
      
      8. Write an autonomous
        
        
        In AUTO the gamepad is switched off and your code has 30 seconds. Open the example Autonomous and read it top to bottom: it uses encoders to drive exact distances and the IMU to turn exact angles. Run it, then change the distances and watch what happens. Use the Start spot list to try other starting places.
        9. 
      
      9. Play a full match
        
        
        Choose a Match autonomous and a Match TeleOp, then press Play full match. The clock runs 30 s of AUTO, an 8 s pause, then 2:00 of TELEOP. With 60 s left your remaining NECTAR arrives and NECTAR may go into FLOWERS. Finish by parking in your LOADING ZONE. The Score sheet shows where every point came from.
        10. 
      
      10. Take it to the real robot
        
        
        The Java here is written the way the FTC SDK expects, so you can paste it into OnBot Java or Android Studio. Then tune it: real wheels slip, batteries sag, and every number (launch power, inches, degrees) will need small changes. That tuning is part of the competition.
        11. 
    
      

    
      Java the simulator understands
      
    
        
      
      • LinearOpMode with runOpMode(), and iterative OpMode with init() and loop().
        • 
      
      • waitForStart(), opModeIsActive(), sleep(ms), idle(), getRuntime(), ElapsedTime.
        • 
      
      • hardwareMap.get(DcMotor.class, "name") for DcMotor, Servo, IMU and DistanceSensor.
        • 
      
      • Motors: setPower, setDirection, setMode (all four run modes), setTargetPosition, getCurrentPosition, isBusy, setZeroPowerBehavior, setVelocity.
        • 
      
      • Servos: setPosition, getPosition, setDirection. Sensors: imu.getRobotYawPitchRollAngles().getYaw(AngleUnit.DEGREES), imu.resetYaw(), distance.getDistance(DistanceUnit.INCH).
        • 
      
      • gamepad1 sticks, triggers, bumpers, D-pad and A/B/X/Y. telemetry.addData, addLine, update.
        • 
      
      • Variables, if/else, while, for, your own methods, arrays, Math, Range.clip, String.format.
        • 
      
      • Not supported yet: switch, your own classes and enums, lambdas, and vision (AprilTags).
        • 
    
      

    
      How the motors and servos are imitated
      
    
        
      
      • Motors take time to speed up and slow down, and they brake or coast depending on ZeroPowerBehavior.
        • 
      
      • Every motor has an encoder with the real ticks-per-turn for the motor you chose. RUN_TO_POSITION works like the real thing, and forgetting setTargetPosition first gives the same error.
        • 
      
      • Mecanum wheels strafe about 15% slower than they drive, like real ones.
        • 
      
      • Servos take about a third of a second to swing end to end. The launcher wheel loses speed with every shot and needs a moment to recover.
        • 
      
      • The lift has hard stops at 0 and 26 inches. The robot can hold 4 pieces at most.
        • 
    
      

    
      Safety and privacy
      
    
      
      
      Nothing you do here leaves your device. The site has no accounts, no passwords, no server, no database, no ads and no tracking. It loads no code or fonts from other websites.
      
      
        
        
      • Your Java is read by a small interpreter built for this site. It is never run as real browser code, so a program can only touch the pretend robot, never your files, camera, or other websites.
        • 
        
      • Programs that loop forever cannot freeze the page: each one gets a limited number of steps per frame, and Stop always works.
        • 
        
      • Everything you type is shown as plain text, so pasted code cannot inject scripts into the page.
        • 
        
      • Saved programs live only in this browser's storage. They are checked and cleaned every time they are loaded.
        • 
      
      
      
      No website can promise it is impossible to attack. This one keeps the risk low by having nothing to steal and nowhere to send it.

      Lesson 1: what a block is
      
        
      A block is one instruction for the robot. Blocks run from top to bottom, one after another. The colour tells you what kind of instruction it is.
      
        
        
          
      • CONTROL decides when and how often things happen: repeat, if / else, wait.
        • 
          
      • DRIVE moves the wheels: drive, strafe, turn, stop.
        • 
          
      • TOOLS works the intake, launcher, lift, and any single motor or servo.
        • 
          
      • TELEMETRY shows a message on the Driver Station screen.
        •

      Lesson 1: the shape of an OpMode
      
        
      Every program has the same three parts: set up, wait for START, then loop.
      

      @TeleOp(name = "My First OpMode")
public class MyFirstOpMode extends LinearOpMode {

    private DcMotor frontLeft;

    @Override
    public void runOpMode() {
        // 1. Set up: connect to the hardware by name.
        frontLeft = hardwareMap.get(DcMotor.class, "frontLeft");

        telemetry.addData("Status", "Ready");
        telemetry.update();

        // 2. Wait for the driver to press START.
        waitForStart();

        // 3. Loop until STOP is pressed.
        while (opModeIsActive()) {
            frontLeft.setPower(-gamepad1.left_stick_y);
            telemetry.addData("Power", frontLeft.getPower());
            telemetry.update();
        }
    }
}
      
      
      Lesson 2: reading the colours
      
        
        
          
      • Keywords such as if, while and double are words Java already knows.
        • 
          
      • Types such as DcMotor and Servo start with a capital letter.
        • 
          
      • Methods are actions, and always have brackets after them: setPower(1).
        • 
          
      • "Text" is in quotes. Numbers and true / false share a colour.
        • 
          
      • // Comments are notes for people. The robot ignores them.
        • 
          
      • Brackets come in pairs. Each pair shares a colour, so you can see which { belongs to which }. A red bracket has no partner.
        • 
        
      
      
      Lesson 3: variables
      
        
      A variable is a named box that holds a value. You must say what type of value goes in it.
      
        
      
          
      TypeHoldsExample
      
          
            
      intWhole numbersint shots = 3;
      
            
      doubleDecimal numbersdouble power = 0.75;
      
            
      booleantrue or falseboolean ready = true;
      
            
      StringTextString name = "Hydra";
      
                
        
      
        
      Watch out: 7 / 2 is 3 because both are whole numbers. Write 7 / 2.0 to get 3.5.
      
      
      Lesson 4: motors, servos and the gamepad
      

      // Motors take a power from -1.0 (full reverse) to 1.0 (full forward).
intake.setPower(1.0);

// Servos take a position from 0.0 to 1.0.
gate.setPosition(0.5);

// Buttons are true or false. Sticks and triggers are numbers.
if (gamepad1.a) {
    feeder.setPosition(1.0);
} else {
    feeder.setPosition(0.0);
}
launcher.setPower(gamepad1.right_trigger);
      
        
      The names (intake, gate, feeder) must match the Robot configuration table on the Build Bot tab exactly, capital letters included.
      
      
      Lesson 5: fixing errors
      
        
      
          
      The message saysWhat to check
      
          
            
      Missing semicolonEvery instruction ends with ;
      
            
      I don't know what "x" isSpelling and capitals, or you forgot to declare the variable.
      
            
      "m" is nullYou used a motor before hardwareMap.get(...) gave it a value.
      
            
      There is no device named…The name in quotes must match the Build Bot table.
      
            
      The class is missing its closing }Count your brackets. The colours help: every opener needs a closer of the same colour.
      
            
      The robot spins instead of driving straightReverse the left motors with setDirection, as the examples do.

      Quick reference, in our own words
      
    
      Based on manual version TU04 and Field Setup Guide V1.0. If anything here disagrees with the manual, the manual is right.
      

    
      The match
      
    
        
      
      • Two alliances of two robots each. This simulator gives you one robot on an empty field.
        • 
      
      • AUTO 30 seconds: robots run on code only. Then an 8 second pause with no powered movement. Then TELEOP 2 minutes with drivers.
        • 
      
      • Robots collect POLLEN and NECTAR, launch them into their HIVE to tip it, place them in FLOWERS and GARDENS, and finish in their LOADING ZONE.
        • 
    
      

    
      The pieces and the field
      
    
        
      
      • POLLEN: yellow balls about 2.8 in across, 40 per match. NECTAR: red or blue balls about 3.6 in across, 8 per alliance.
        • 
      
      • At the start: 4 POLLEN in each FLOWER, 4 in each GARDEN, 4 pre-loaded in each robot, 3 NECTAR in each alliance's upward CELL, and 5 NECTAR waiting with each alliance's human player.
        • 
      
      • HIVE: each alliance has one, with two CELLS on a pivot. One CELL faces up at a time. Field staff set it to tip at 8 POLLEN, or at 3 POLLEN plus 3 NECTAR.
        • 
      
      • FLOWER: four on the walls. Pieces go in at the top (about 21.5 in high). Only POLLEN may be taken out, and only from the bottom.
        • 
      
      • GARDEN: a thin taped strip in one corner per alliance (red on tile A1, blue on tile F6). LOADING ZONE: a 23 in by 11 in taped box against each alliance wall (red on A5, blue on F2).
        • 
    
      

    
      Points
      
    
      
      
      What you doAUTOTELEOP
      
      
        
      LEAVE: robot no longer touching the wall at the end of AUTO3–
      
        
      PARK: robot at least partly in its LOADING ZONE at the end of the period55
      
        
      HIVE TIP, each time2020
      
        
      Each piece left in your upward CELL at the end–2
      
        
      Each piece in a FLOWER you own–2
      
        
      Bottom NECTAR bonus (your NECTAR is the lowest in a FLOWER)–5
      
        
      Each piece in your GARDEN at the end–1
      
            
    
      
    
        
      
      • You own a FLOWER when the highest NECTAR in it is your colour. The owner scores every piece in it, whoever put it there.
        • 
      
      • Ranking points at most events: SWARM for 16 combined LEAVE + PARK points, POLLINATOR 1 for 4 tips, POLLINATOR 2 for 7 tips, plus 3 for a win or 1 for a tie.
        • 
    
      

    
      Rules that catch new teams
      
    
        
      
      • G407: control at most 4 pieces at once. G408: never control the other alliance's NECTAR.
        • 
      
      • G410: NECTAR may only go into FLOWERS in the last 60 seconds. Earlier is a major foul for each one.
        • 
      
      • G417 / G418: only LAUNCHING into the upward CELL may tip a HIVE; do not push or hold it. Use FLOWERS only from the top (in) and bottom (POLLEN out).
        • 
      
      • G402: in AUTO, stay on your own half (columns A–C for red, D–F for blue). G403 / G404: no powered movement in the 8 second pause or after the final buzzer.
        • 
      
      • G426: your human player may enter one NECTAR each time your HIVE tips, and all the rest once 60 seconds remain.
        • 
      
      • R102: the robot starts inside an 18 in cube. R105: it may grow to 18 × 24 in and 29 in tall. R503: at most 8 motors and 8 servos.
        • 
    
      

    
      What this simulator simplifies
      
    
        
      
      • The view is flat, from above. Heights are handled with numbers (lift height, FLOWER opening) instead of 3D.
        • 
      
      • The exact FLOWER positions along the walls are our best estimate from the public documents. Measure the real field, or use the official CAD model, before you rely on distances.
        • 
      
      • The bottom piece in a FLOWER sits in the retrieval opening and is treated as not scoring. Tipping is worked out from the 8 POLLEN and 3 + 3 calibration points.
        • 
      
      • There are no partner or opponent robots, no referees and no penalties in the score. Fouls appear as warnings in the Match log.

      • your appearance choices (white or black mode and font colour) and whether sound is on.

      Accessibility
      
      
      We want every student to be able to use this site. This is what is in place today, and what is not yet.
      
      
        
        
      • Alt text. Every picture has a text description: the field view, the robot preview and the Home page artwork. Decorative shapes and emoji are hidden from screen readers so they are not read out as noise.
        • 
        
      • Keyboard. Every button, list and text box can be reached with Tab and shows a clear outline when focused. The robot can be driven entirely from the keyboard.
        • 
        
      • Colour. The site follows your light or dark setting and keeps text contrast high. Game pieces are also named in text, so colour is never the only clue in the score sheet or match log.
        • 
        
      • Motion. If your device is set to reduce motion, the tool that follows the cursor on the Home page is switched off.
        • 
        
      • Sound. Sound effects are extras: nothing depends on hearing them, every one has a matching message on screen, and they can be switched off under Appearance.
        • 
        
      • Small screens. The layout stacks into one column on phones, and the on-screen gamepad works by touch.
        • 
        
      • Known limits. The field is a live picture, so a screen reader cannot follow the robot as it moves. The Telemetry, Motors & servos, Score sheet and Match log panels give the same information as text. Dragging the on-screen sticks needs a pointer; the keyboard keys do the same job.
        • 
      
      
      
      If something is hard to use, tell a member of Team 18629 – Hydra so it can be fixed.
